The headline is simple enough. From 28 July 2026, if you own private property you no longer have to wait fifteen months before buying an HDB resale flat on the open market.

What almost nobody is pointing out is that this did not replace the old route. It added a second one. And the new, convenient one has a price attached that the announcement does not mention.

## You now have two ways to do this

**Sell the condo first, then buy the flat.** This is what everyone did before, minus the fifteen-month wait. You move twice, or you rent for a few months. Nothing else happens.

**Buy the flat first, then sell the condo.** You move once. No rental, no storage, no living out of boxes. This is the new option, and it is obviously the nicer one.

Here is what makes the second route a bet rather than a convenience.

## The six-month clock

For however long you own both properties, you own two homes. Buying a second property normally means paying Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty — 20% for a citizen, 30% for a permanent resident.

You are not asked for it upfront, because HDB requires you to sell the condo within six months of getting the keys anyway. So it is waived from the start.

Waived is not the same as gone. It is waived **on condition** that you actually sell in time. On a S$650,000 flat, a citizen who does not is looking at a **S$130,000** bill.

There is a second sting if you bought your condo less than four years ago. Selling inside that window triggers Seller's Stamp Duty at 16%, 12%, 8% or 4% depending on the year — so a deadline you did not choose can force a sale that carries its own tax.

Six months sounds generous until you are the one waiting for an offer. HDB's own price index has fallen for two consecutive quarters, which is precisely why the rule was relaxed — a market that is cooling is not one that guarantees a quick sale.

## So which route is right?

That depends on something no calculator can tell you: how confident you are that your property sells inside six months.

If it is the kind of unit that moves in weeks, buying first is a genuine gift — one move instead of two, and no rental in between. If it is unusual, over-priced, or in a segment that is currently slow, then selling first costs you some inconvenience and removes a six-figure risk entirely.

The rule change did not make the decision for you. It gave you an option, and options have prices.

One thing it will show you that surprises people: the CPF refund is mostly a non-event. The money goes back into your CPF account, then comes straight out again to pay for the flat. It only strands cash if you are trading down far enough that the refund is bigger than the new place can absorb — or if you are under 55, when you cannot withdraw it at all.

## Before you commit to buying first

Ask an agent how long comparable units in your project have actually taken to sell this year. Not the asking prices — the time on the market.

That number, not the tax rate, is what decides whether the convenient route is worth taking.
